The <a href="/veta-trading-platform/platform/services/">Market Simulator</a> (`market-sim`, port 5000) generates synthetic prices for every instrument in the platform. This page documents the rationale behind its price model constants and the conventions used across the generated instrument universe, the detail a reader needs once they start tuning the model or adding a new asset class.

## Price model

Prices follow a geometric Brownian motion (GBM) step with mean reversion, sector correlation, and regime-based drift.

<CodeBlock
  path="backend/src/market-sim/price-engine.ts"
  region="docs:gbm-constants"
  title="Price model constants"
/>

- **`TICKS_PER_DAY`**: the simulator emits 4 ticks/second across a 390-minute trading day (390 × 60 × 4 = 93,600). Daily volatility is scaled by `1/√TICKS_PER_DAY` so each tick's shock accumulates into a realistic distribution when aggregated into 1-minute candles.
- **`MEAN_REVERSION_SPEED`**: the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck reversion constant. At 0.0002, a price displaced from its anchor reverts over roughly 5,000 ticks (about 1.25 trading hours), which keeps prices wandering realistically without drifting away from their seeded reference indefinitely.
- **`SECTOR_CORRELATION`**: the fraction of each tick's random shock that is shared with the asset's sector (the rest is idiosyncratic). At 0.35, sector-wide moves are visible on the heatmap without every stock in a sector moving in lockstep.

## Async prewarm

<CodeBlock
  path="backend/src/market-sim/price-engine.ts"
  region="docs:prewarm-async"
  title="Chunked prewarm"
/>

`prewarmPrices` runs the GBM step synchronously for thousands of ticks so a freshly seeded market starts with realistic intraday drift instead of a flat line. At the full expanded instrument universe size, running that loop synchronously in one go can take long enough to block the event loop, which starves HTTP handling, including the `/health` check other services poll. `prewarmPricesAsync` breaks the same work into `PREWARM_CHUNK_TICKS`-sized chunks and yields to the event loop between chunks, so the server stays responsive while prewarming completes in the background.

## Reset-in-progress guard

<CodeBlock
  path="backend/src/market-sim/price-engine.ts"
  region="docs:reset-in-progress"
  title="Reset guard"
/>

`resetPriceEngine` (used by [scenario replay](/veta-trading-platform/platform/scenarios/) reseeding and the `/seed` route) reruns the chunked prewarm, which yields to the event loop repeatedly. The live tick loop in `market-sim.ts` runs on its own `setInterval` and would otherwise be free to fire a tick between prewarm chunks, mutating `marketData` and consuming RNG state mid-reset. `isResetInProgress()` lets the tick loop skip itself while a reset is underway, so a reset's outcome does not depend on how ticks happen to interleave with prewarm chunks.

## Session timing

<CodeBlock
  path="backend/src/lib/time-scale.ts"
  region="docs:intraday-volume-curve"
  title="Intraday volume curve"
/>

Trading volume is not flat across a session, it is higher at the open and close and lower around midday. `intradayVolumeFactor` models this with `cos²(π·x)`, where `x` is the fraction of the trading day elapsed: the curve peaks at 1.0 at the open and close and troughs at 0.3 at midday, giving a believable volume shape without a lookup table.

## Instrument metadata conventions

Each asset class module (`sp500-assets.ts`, `fx-assets.ts`, `commodity-assets.ts`, `bond-assets.ts`) defines the same `AssetDef` shape but with class-specific conventions for what the fields mean. For curated equities, `RawMeta.lotSize` is optional: most names auto-derive their round lot size from `initialPrice` (ETFs and high-price stocks trade in lots of 1, mid-price in lots of 10, everything else in lots of 100), and the curated entry only needs to set `lotSize` explicitly when a symbol should deviate from that derivation.

### Commodities

<Source path="backend/src/market-sim/commodity-assets.ts" region="docs:commodity-conventions" />

Commodity futures are modelled as front-month continuous contracts. Price is USD per standard contract unit (barrel, MMBtu, troy ounce, and so on). Volatility sits between FX and speculative equities. `lotSize` is always 1, since OMS quantities for commodities are integer contracts, not shares. `exchange` collapses the real CME/NYMEX/CBOT venues onto a single simulated MIC per commodity.

### FX

<Source path="backend/src/market-sim/fx-assets.ts" region="docs:fx-conventions" />

FX price is quote-currency units per one unit of base currency. Volatility is lower than equities, matching real spot FX behaviour. `lotSize` is 1000 (a standard FX lot expressed in base units for display). `exchange` is fixed to `XCME`: spot FX is OTC in reality, but the simulator needs a single MIC to slot into the same `AssetDef` shape as exchange-traded instruments.

### Equity-derived heuristics

Most S&P 500-style names get curated metadata (`CURATED_META`) for the most-traded symbols, but the long tail of generated equities needs its metadata derived algorithmically from price and volatility alone:

<CodeBlock
  path="backend/src/market-sim/sp500-assets.ts"
  region="docs:derive-heuristics"
  title="Metadata derivation heuristics"
/>

- **Market cap**: approximated as `√(dailyVolume / 1000) × 100` million shares outstanding, on the assumption that higher average daily volume correlates with a larger float and a larger company.
- **Beta**: `volatility / 0.01`, treating 1% daily volatility (roughly SPY's historical level) as beta 1.0.
- **Dividend yield / P/E**: sector-keyed base rates, zeroed out above a volatility threshold on the assumption that high-volatility names are growth or speculative names that do not pay dividends or trade on trailing earnings.

These are heuristics for generating a plausible, internally consistent synthetic universe. They are not calibrated against real market data and should not be read as financial modelling.

## Fixed income data placement

<Source path="backend/src/market-sim/bond-universe.ts" region="docs:bond-universe-shared" />

Bond definitions (curated on-the-run issues plus a generated set of off-the-run UST and investment-grade corporate bonds) live under `shared/`, not `backend/src/market-sim/`, so that the frontend order ticket can import the same bond universe directly instead of maintaining a duplicate copy that could drift out of sync.

## `venueBooks` is excluded from the Kafka feed

<Source path="backend/src/market-sim/market-sim.ts" region="docs:venuebooks-sniper-only" />

The `market.ticks` Kafka topic carries a diff of the tick payload (see the [market data pipeline](/veta-trading-platform/platform/architecture/) for the overall flow), but `venueBooks` (per-venue order book snapshots) is deliberately stripped before publishing. Only the SNIPER algo's smart-order-router consumes per-venue books, and it reads them directly over the market-sim WebSocket via `market-sim-client.ts`, not from Kafka. At full instrument-universe size, including `venueBooks` in every Kafka message risks exceeding the broker's maximum message size for no consumer that needs it.

<Source path="backend/src/market-sim/tick-diff.ts" region="docs:symbols-needing-fresh-book" />

`symbolsNeedingFreshBook` exists so the tick loop can skip the expensive per-symbol order-book construction entirely for symbols that the diff would discard anyway, rather than building every symbol's book on every tick and throwing most of it away afterwards. It unions the main order book's "last published price" baseline with every venue's independently tracked baseline, because those baselines can drift apart over time even though they share the same `BOOK_MATERIAL_BPS` materiality threshold.
