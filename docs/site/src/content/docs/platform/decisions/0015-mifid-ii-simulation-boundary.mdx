---
title: "ADR 0015: MiFID II controls are a simulation with a stated boundary"
description: "Records what the platform implements for MiFID II order record keeping, clock synchronisation, transaction reporting and algorithmic trading controls, and what it deliberately does not claim."
---

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** 2026-11-09

## Context

VETA is a simulated trading platform that aims to look and behave like a regulated firm's
stack. MiFID II shapes the data a real firm keeps: who decided, which algorithm executed,
when it happened and how long the record survives. Building those shapes makes the
platform more realistic and exercises real engineering problems, such as tamper evidence
and clock traceability.

It does not make the platform a regulated firm. There is no authorisation, no legal entity
identifier of its own, no approved reporting mechanism connection and no regulator.
Claiming compliance would be false, and quietly implementing half a control is worse than
documenting its absence.

## Decision

Implement the data shapes, controls, reports and evidence for four areas, test them, and
state their limits in the documentation.

| Area | Implemented | Not implemented |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Order record keeping (RTS 24) | Regulatory identifiers on orders and fills, an append-only hash-chained order record, five year object-locked export | Legal retention guarantees, off-site storage SLAs |
| Clock synchronisation (RTS 25) | Microsecond timestamps, SNTP drift measurement, alerts at 1 ms | Traceable UTC source, 100 microsecond accuracy evidence |
| Transaction reporting (RTS 22) | A documented field subset as JSON and CSV for compliance users | Submission to an approved reporting mechanism, ISIN, natural person identifiers, amendments |
| Algorithmic trading (RTS 6) | Algo registry with versions, kill switch that stops child orders, risk engine disabled audit event, scenario runs recorded with algo version and commit, a controls report | Annual self-assessment, market abuse surveillance, real-time monitoring staff |

Specific choices:

- **No new secrets for the regulatory record.** It reuses the audit writer role and
  connection, which keeps the OpenBao surface unchanged.
- **Reports are generated on demand from the immutable record**, not stored, so the record
  is the single source of truth.
- **Cancellation is enforced at the EMS.** Algorithms do not consume cancellation events,
  so the execution management service refuses child orders whose parent was cancelled.
  This covers every strategy at one point instead of nine.
- **Identifiers fall back to the user id** when no client or decision maker code is
  configured, and the firm LEI stays empty unless `FIRM_LEI` is set. Nothing is invented.

## Consequences

- The platform can demonstrate each control end to end, and tests prove the immutability,
  suppression and access rules.
- Every page that describes these controls states the simulation boundary, so a reader
  cannot mistake the output for a compliance artefact.
- Closing a gap, such as adding ISIN support or a real reporting submission, is additive:
  the record already carries the payloads needed.
- A kill still lets an algorithm schedule slices that are then dropped. That is wasted work
  rather than a market impact, and a future change could stop the algorithm earlier.
